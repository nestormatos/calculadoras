Add up your daily caffeine against evidence-based limits, and estimate the alcohol in your drinks in standard units — with a practical water suggestion and none of the usual myths.

Part A — Caffeine

Caffeine values are approximate (Mayo Clinic figures, mg per 8 fl oz unless noted). Add every caffeinated drink you have today.

Your caffeine limit

Caffeinated drinks still count toward your fluid intake. In Beverage Hydration Index studies (Maughan et al., 2016), coffee, tea and cola produced the same urine volume as water over 4 hours — so this tool never subtracts their volume from your hydration.

Part B — Alcohol

Estimate the pure alcohol in your drinks, expressed as standard drinks, with a practical water-pairing suggestion.

Standard drink definition

The US standard drink is 14 g of pure alcohol; many other countries use 10 g.

This tool does not calculate blood alcohol or driving safety — if you have been drinking, do not drive. And while water between drinks helps you pace yourself, it does not prevent intoxication or hangovers.

The water suggestion is a practical public-health rule of thumb — about one glass of water (8 fl oz) per standard drink — not a medical target. Track the rest of your day with the Water Tracker.

How the estimates are built

Caffeine

Total mg = Σ (mg per serving × servings)
12 fl oz can = 8 fl oz value × 1.5
Limits: 400 mg/day (adults), 200 mg/day (pregnancy)

Alcohol

ethanol (g) = volume (fl oz) × ABV ÷ 100 × 23.3335 × servings
standard drinks = ethanol (g) ÷ 14 g (US) or 10 g
suggested water = standard drinks × 8 fl oz

Studies show that moderate caffeine intake does not dehydrate you: in the Beverage Hydration Index work of Maughan and colleagues (2016), coffee, tea and cola hydrated participants about as well as water over four hours. The caffeine limits come from the FDA (healthy adults) and ACOG (pregnancy). The water-per-drink figure is a practical guide used in public-health campaigns, not a clinical rule. Disclaimer

General guidance for healthy people — not medical advice. If you have kidney, heart or liver disease, or you have been told to restrict fluids or sodium, follow your doctor's instructions. Caffeine values are approximations and alcohol content varies by brand and pour; this tool cannot estimate blood alcohol concentration. If you are pregnant, managing a medical condition, or taking medication, ask a healthcare professional about safe caffeine and alcohol limits.
