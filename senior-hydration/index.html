A calm daily tracker for adults 65+ and the people who care for them — a steady per-hour pace, one-tap logging and gentle reminders.

Set up the day

According to ESPEN, older women should drink at least 54 fl oz and older men at least 68 fl oz per day — unless a doctor has prescribed a fluid restriction.

Doctor-prescribed fluid restriction?

Common in heart or kidney conditions.

Follow the amount your doctor prescribed — the ESPEN minimums do not apply when fluids are restricted.

    How the pace is built

    Target & pace

    target: ESPEN minimums — women 54 fl oz, men 68 fl oz
    pace = target ÷ waking hours
    expected = pace × hours since waking
    behind = expected − logged (never below 0)

    Signs that do NOT work in older adults

    According to ESPEN (grade-A evidence), urine color, thirst, dry mouth and skin elasticity are not reliable dehydration indicators in people over 65 — thirst fades with age and the other signs mislead. That is why this tracker uses intake against a target, not symptoms.

    Practical tips from ESPEN for caregivers: keep drinks visible and within easy reach, offer a variety (water, milk, juice, soup, tea), offer small amounts often rather than large amounts rarely, and make sure the bathroom is quick and easy to reach — fear of not getting to the toilet in time makes many older people deliberately drink less.

    Seek medical attention if the person is more confused or drowsy than usual, urinates very little, has fever, vomiting or diarrhea, or cannot drink.

    Disclaimer

    General guidance for healthy people — not medical advice. If you have kidney, heart or liver disease, or you have been told to restrict fluids or sodium, follow your doctor's instructions. Drinking too much can also be dangerous: it can lower blood sodium (hyponatremia).
